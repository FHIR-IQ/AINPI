# The directory got smaller, and almost nobody fell out of it

The US government publishes a list of everyone who provides health care, the
National Provider Directory. On 7 October it published a new version. We
reloaded all of it, re-ran every check we publish, and compared each number
with the August release.

The short version: the file shrank by a quarter, and the share of clinicians
it describes did not shrink with it. Health plans arrived in large numbers,
and the directory gives none of them a web address.

## A quarter smaller

| | August | October | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| All records | 32,468,908 | 24,761,939 | −23.7% |
| Clinicians | 7,373,232 | 7,481,907 | +1.5% |
| Where-they-work records | 16,545,158 | 11,056,751 | −33.2% |
| Organizations | 4,402,671 | 2,058,500 | −53.2% |
| Places | 2,535,686 | 2,558,069 | +0.9% |
| Web addresses | 1,128,169 | 1,140,836 | +1.1% |
| Links between organizations | 483,992 | 465,876 | −3.7% |

Two lines carry nearly all of the drop.

**Organizations halved because tax records left.** In August, 2,199,519 of
the organization records were labelled only as tax-ID records ("ein"),
with no provider type. In October there are 79,721. Organizations labelled as providers
fell too, but by about a tenth: 2,202,028 to 1,976,702.

**Where-they-work records fell by 5.5 million, and coverage held.** In
August the directory more than doubled these records. Most of the new ones
went to people who already had one. October takes
much of that back. The average clinician with a workplace listed now has
about 3.2 records, down from about 4.7.

What matters is whether people lost their workplace. They mostly did not. In
Pennsylvania, where we track this most closely, the share of clinicians with
a workplace listed went from 43.7% to 44.0%. Nationally it is 32.5%.

## Health plans arrived. None has a web address

In August the directory named 27 health insurers for the first time. In
October it names 997, and 7,575 individual health plans, each pointing at the
insurer that runs it.

None of the 997 insurers has a web address in the directory.

There are now 50 addresses in the file that look like insurer provider
directories, up from one. We checked both ways: none of them is attached to
an insurer, and no insurer points at one. So the file now says who the
insurers are and, separately, lists some of their directories, without
connecting the two.

Federal rules have required Medicare Advantage, Medicaid and CHIP plans to
publish these directories since 2021.
We test one that answers a plain request today. It is still not in the
directory.

## Two things got worse

**Phone numbers.** In August, all but about 1,900 clinicians had a phone
number listed. In October, 168,778 have none anywhere. On the clinician's own
record, 176,084 have no phone: about 111,800 list only a fax number and 64,243
list nothing at all. For 7,306 of those, a workplace record supplies one.
Coverage is still 97.7%, so the drop is small.

**Reaching patient records through a clinician's practice.** We can connect
15.8% of Pennsylvania clinicians to a web address where software could request
patient records from their practice. In
August it was 18.7%. That covers every route we know,
including the vendors' own public lists. We used the same method for both
releases, so this drop comes from the data, not from a change in how we
count.

The professions at the bottom of the table kept rising. Four near the top
fell: nurse practitioners and physician assistants lost almost five points,
chiropractors more than five, eye doctors about four and foot doctors almost
three. We do not know why yet, and we are not going to
guess in print.

## Two new kinds of record, one of them empty

The October file again carries two record types we do not load into our
warehouse. One describes health plans. Every one of the 7,575 names its insurer,
but only 233 say what kind of plan it is or which network it uses.

The other is meant to describe services, such as a clinic offering
vaccinations. There are 54,445 of these. None says what the service is: no
name, no type, no specialty. Fewer than 1 in 100 says where it is offered,
and one names a provider. As shipped, it records that two parties are linked
and nothing about the service itself.

## What held steady

- Of the web addresses software can actually use, 14.6% say which
  organization they belong to. It was 14.7% in August.
- The software vendors' own public lists name the owner of 64.4% of the rest.
  One vendor moved its list during our run; we found the new location and
  used it.
- Social Security numbers in the file: still zero.
- Malformed provider ID numbers: zero, out of 9.5 million checked. August had
  four.

## A correction

For two days before this update, our federal exclusion check reported no
excluded providers at all, and our high-risk list dropped every provider
flagged only by that check. That was wrong. The government
changed how its exclusion file marks an empty field, from a run of zeros to a
single zero, and our check then read every exclusion as lifted. It returned
an empty answer instead of an error, which is the failure we most try to
guard against.

It is fixed, and the loader now refuses a file in which most exclusions look
lifted. The October answer: 7,799 of the 8,924 excluded providers who have an
NPI appear in the directory.

## We kept the August release

The government serves only the latest version of the directory. When October
arrived, August disappeared from its site. We keep every release since May
in one free, public archive on Databricks Marketplace, so anyone can check the comparisons above against the original records.
